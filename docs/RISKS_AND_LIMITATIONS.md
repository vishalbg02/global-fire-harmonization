# Risks and Limitations

Likelihood and impact: L (low) · M (medium) · H (high).

## 1. Scientific risks

| # | Risk | L | I | Mitigation / fallback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | One conversion ratio is invalid across fire regimes | H (certain if done naively) | H | Partial pooling by stratum; Fallback A = fixed regional ratios |
| S2 | Ratios not stationary (fire sizes change within a region) | M | M | Calibration never re-fitted per year; large-fire diagnostic (P1); disclosed |
| S3 | Aqua drift contaminates the reference scale after ~2021–22 | H | M | Drift measured empirically; Aqua removed from the combination after the cutoff; wider intervals shown |
| S4 | Intervals too narrow (witness errors correlated) | H | M | Disagreement inflation; coverage test D1; disclosed |
| S5 | Regional burning-time shifts (e.g. Punjab) cause false "decline" | H (where present) | M | `DIURNAL_CAVEAT`; never claim burning fell there |
| S6 | Real anomalies suppressed by calibration | H (measured, see S19) | H | Tests A1, A2 and CTRL ran and **all three FAIL** for method 0.1.0: very large events are under-stated by 20% to 65% (A1 and A2, S19) and verdicts near the satellite-change dates can differ from the Aqua-only series (CTRL, S21). Calibration is fitted only on overlap years; the failures are disclosed, not repaired (v0.2 not adopted, D-030); see S19, S20 and S21 |
| S7 | Baseline depends on harmonization | M | M | Sensitivity test S1 with an Aqua-measured baseline |
| S8 | Results sensitive to grid size or thresholds chosen after seeing results | M | H | Pre-registration at G1 |
| S9 | Validation tests fail | M | M | Publish the failures and narrow claims. Honesty is part of the product |
| S10 | MODIS `version` inconsistency hides a processing change | L–M | M | Investigated in T-007: no step in type 0 or kept counts relative to S-NPP at the 2018 or 2023 version changes; static-source share rises about 1.5 times in 2018 and low-confidence share and mean FRP rise in 2023 (cause open); nothing dropped; flag or adjust is the Lead's decision |
| S11 | Static-source mask for NRT is imperfect (NRT has no `type`) | H (certain) | M | Measured on held-out years: removes 88% of static detections and wrongly removes 0.55% of fire detections at the proposed setting; residual static sources remain in NRT months, which are already provisional; the setting (0.05° cells, share at least 0.5, at least 10 detections) was approved by the Lead 2026-11-14 |
| S12 | Night-source mask (D-025) misses flares lit on fewer than 1% of nights or in fewer than 10 months (residual rows in the Permian and Bakken: `TENA_b048_030` NOAA-20 DJF at 0.14 of global, `TENA_b055_030` S-NPP JJA at 0.15) | M | L | Held-out cost is at most 0.94% of VIIRS night type 0 detections (2024 to 2025); the residual rows are visible in the sanity table; approved by the Lead 2026-11-14 |
| S13 | Eight Bolivian cells (Chiquitano, 20 to 15 S, 62.5 to 60 W) are still flagged by the night-source rule. Cause **OPEN**: no type 2 or 3 labels, a Jul to Nov peak, 4,712 type 0 detections over 14 years | L | L | Negligible volume against the dry-forest total (Jul to Oct holds 49.8% of the 220 cells flagged before condition 4); accepted as a documented limitation by the Lead 2026-11-14 |
| S14 | Fagradalsfjall (`EURO_b061_063`) NOAA-20 JJA night ratio stays `LOCAL` at 8.6 times global: the 2021 eruption ran from March to September, so no single month passes the burst rule's 50% | L | L | One stratum and season; a season-year test would catch it but would move about 23% of `LOCAL` rows (3,345 of 14,422), so it is not applied; accepted by the Lead 2026-11-14. Treat night conversions there with care |
| S15 | Low night ratios in savanna and agricultural fire regions (SE Asia DJF and SON, southern Africa JJA, northern Africa DJF) are kept as `LOCAL`. They are a **hypothesis**, not a verified fact: VIIRS (375 m) sees many small night fires that MODIS (1 km) misses | M | M | Accepted by the Lead as real regional differences (2026-11-14); to be confirmed in T-012 and test E1 by region; if they are artefacts, night conversions in those regions would be too low or too high |
| S16 | Interval coverage on the holdout years (T-012, 2017 to 2021) is inside 80% to 97% when pooled (regions 0.818, countries 0.811; totals 0.847 and 0.826) but below 80% for night units alone (0.750 regions, 0.794 countries), and about half of the individual regions and countries are below 80% | M | M | The `phi` and `kappa` variance (T-013) lifted pooled coverage from 0.62 and 0.78; test D1 (T-022) is the final check; night and small-area verdicts need wider thresholds or a `WIDE_INTERVAL` caveat; `phi` and `kappa` are in-sample lower bounds |
| S17 | Aqua drift: the pre-registered test (global day, S-NPP) detects none, but night-time Aqua / converted VIIRS falls from about 2024-05 (S-NPP) to 2024-09 (NOAA-20) in both witnesses. The cutoff of D-005 (Aqua night excluded from 2024-05, Aqua day kept) **was chosen after seeing the results**, as a documented deviation from the registered rule; it uses the earlier of two overlapping onsets; its cause (Aqua, processing or VIIRS) is open | M | M | Approved by the Lead 2026-11-14 (D-005); months from 2024-05 carry `AQUA_DRIFT_ERA`; the estimates of 2024-05 to 2026-10 move by +0.7% to +1.2% on average (Brazil, Australia, Indonesia) and their sd by -0.6% to -19%; check in T-021 (B3) and T-022; a change of date needs the Lead; one combination method for all months (seam check, PR #45: the totals method differs from it by 0.35% at the median but 28% at the 95th percentile) |
| S18 | Intervals are not valid for regions without their own calibration data. In test E1 (T-025) a left-out region is converted with the global ratio fitted without it, and the 90% interval covers only 10% to 80% of its units; the leave-out error is 2 to 6 times the error with the region seen, and E1 passes against a weak bar (the naive merge) | M | M | Every region in the product is calibrated with its own data, so the E1 situation does not occur in the products; no unseen-region interval now (Lead decision on PR #57, 2026-11-15); do not present intervals for areas outside the calibrated fire regions |
| S19 | Extreme fire months are under-converted: the converted S-NPP estimate is 20% to 40% below Aqua in the peak windows of very large fires (A1 cases C3 to C6: S-NPP over Aqua 0.58 to 0.80) and the intervals do not cover the gap, so the A1 family is FAIL for method 0.1.0 (0 PASS, 4 FAIL, 2 INCONCLUSIVE, D-029). T-020b confirmed by a pre-set rule that the Aqua/VIIRS ratio rises with activity (cell-day saturation is one **hypothesis** for the mechanism). A v0.2 conversion with an activity factor (T-020c, D-030) changed the A1 gaps by only 0.01 to 0.03 in log terms and failed the pre-registered adoption rule (country holdout error 0.1021 against 0.1003), so v0.1 stays. **Test A2 shows the same from the other side:** the same real event planted into a quiet 2010 month as Aqua and into a quiet 2023 month as S-NPP gives harmonized S-NPP at 0.65 (C1), 0.52 (C4) and 0.35 (C6) of harmonized Aqua, 3.6 to 7.5 combined sd lower, with non-overlapping 90% intervals (A2 FAIL, 0 of 3 cases, T-023) | H | M | Disclosed in the app and the methods page; the A1 and A2 FAILs stay in `validation_results.json`; anomalies in very large fires are shown with this caveat; no product claim of magnitude for extreme months; `docs/validation/T-020b-saturation.md`, `docs/validation/T-020c-v02.md`, `docs/validation/T-023-results.md` |
| S20 | **The disagreement flag does not reliably detect a sensor jump** (test B2, FAIL). `WITNESS_DISAGREEMENT` or a disagreement scale of 1.5 or more flagged 84.5% of 200 planted jumps within 3 months (needed 90%): 100% in fire regions but only 69% for single cells. It also fires without any jump: in 25.0% of the unplanted three-month twins (needed under 5%), in 5.6% of all entity-months, and at least once in 27.1% of the entities. A flag therefore means "the satellites disagree", not "a satellite changed" | H (measured) | M | The app words the code only as "The satellites disagree more than expected, so the uncertainty was widened" (never as a sensor fault); the B2 FAIL and its rates stay in `validation_results.json` and on the methods page; the flag is not used as a jump detector in the product or the demo; `docs/validation/T-024-results.md` |
| S21 | **Wrong ABOVE or BELOW verdicts near the satellite-change dates** (test CTRL, FAIL). In the savanna control box C8, 3 of the 50 evaluated months (2011-03, 2011-04, 2012-09; 27 of the 50 were determinable) got an ABOVE or BELOW verdict that the Aqua-only series does not give. Two are before S-NPP exists: the estimates are identical and the verdicts differ only because the harmonized baseline differs from the Aqua-only one. The third, 2012-09, comes from one probability (p_below 0.898 against 0.767) crossing the 0.8 line. With the shared Aqua baseline 2 remain (2012-09 and 2017-10) | M | M | Disclosed on the methods page and in `validation_results.json` (CTRL FAIL). Wording for the app and the demo: an ABOVE or BELOW verdict within 12 months of 2012-01 or 2018-04 is a lead to check against the satellite chart and the Aqua-only series, not a finding. No method change (D-028 kept). A caveat code for change-date months would be a separate decision for the Lead; `docs/validation/T-024-results.md` |
| S22 | **Unprecedented activity in normally quiet cells reads `CANNOT_DETERMINE`.** Under D-028 a cell whose baseline median is below 5 cell-days gets `INSUFFICIENT_BASELINE` even when the month is far above its history. Measured on the T-014 products, for cell-months with an estimate of at least 20 and more than 3 times the baseline p75: **39,682 cell-months worldwide are `CANNOT_DETERMINE`** (all with `INSUFFICIENT_BASELINE`, in 289 distinct months) against 7,942 `ABOVE_NORMAL`. In Australia in 2019-12 (Black Summer) **105 cells are `CANNOT_DETERMINE`** against 4 `ABOVE_NORMAL` under the same condition (15 `ABOVE_NORMAL` cells in all of Australia that month), while the Australian country verdict is `ABOVE_NORMAL` at 328% of normal | H (measured) | M | The rule is kept: no method change, no new caveat code (D-031). The app shows the reason text ("There is too little historical fire activity here to define what is normal"). Entity-level verdicts and custom areas carry these events, and the demo uses them rather than single cells. Counts are reproducible with the condition above on `cell_month_harmonized` |
| S23 | **Country totals for India, Pakistan and China follow FIRMS's attribution in Jammu and Kashmir.** The country of each cell comes from the FIRMS file names (T-009), so cells in the disputed area are assigned to the country FIRMS lists them under, not by India's official boundary. **[VERIFIED]** by `cells.parquet`, cells whose centre lies in 72.5 to 80.5 degrees E and 32.5 to 37.2 degrees N: 42 are assigned to Pakistan, 27 to China, 39 to India, 1 to none (query: `select country_iso3, count(*) from cells.parquet where lon_c between 72.5 and 80.5 and lat_c between 32.5 and 37.2 group by 1`) | M | L | The cells, their harmonized values and verdicts are not affected; only the country (and continent) totals of India, Pakistan and China in `entity_month` and in the app's country views. The Lead decided on PR #74 (D-033) to reassign those cells with Natural Earth's India point-of-view boundary in T-086, after the presentation (regenerate `cells.parquet` and the IND, PAK and CHN rows of `entity_month`, then re-run the contract, number and featured-story checks). Until then the limitation is stated here |

## 3. Performance risks

| # | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Tens of thousands of cells per month in the browser | WebGL grid layer; per-month payloads; 0.5° analysis grid |
| P2 | Custom-area queries over many cells | DuckDB over partitioned Parquet; cap the area size if needed (disclose) |
| P3 | Free hosting cold starts or limits | Local demo primary; static fallback for preset views |
| P4 | Polygon custom area uses the centre rule (T-044): a grid cell belongs when its centre lies inside the polygon (boundary included), so a cell covered 60% with its centre outside is left out, a cell covered 40% with its centre inside is included, and a small polygon may contain no cell centre (422). Limits: 3 to 100 points, inside the world box, no antimeridian crossing, no self-intersection; coordinates kept to 4 decimals; no offline copy | Rule written in `api/README.md` and next to the polygon in the app; tested against a brute-force check on fixtures and real products; the grid is 0.5 degrees, so treat areas smaller than a few cells as approximate |

## 4. Data limitations (permanent; disclose in the app)

- Hotspots ≠ fires, burned area or emissions.
- No detection ≠ no fire (clouds, smoke, canopy, gaps, short fires).
- Snapshots at overpass times only.
- NRT months are provisional; NRT lacks the `type` field.
- The country archives exclude detections outside country boundaries (open ocean, possibly some disputed areas).
- No per-cell overpass or cloud counts in the MVP.
- MODIS ends 2026–27; S-NPP ended 2026-11-02.
